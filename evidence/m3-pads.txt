## What this evidence does NOT prove

- No ROG Ally hardware was available on this host or the tailnet, so the literal exit
  criterion ("On the Ally, a DS4 over BT and an Xbox pad over USB ...") was NOT run.
  The QEMU runs use synthetic uinput pads with the right USB vendor/product, not the
  physical pads, and not Bluetooth.  They prove the udev rules, the session env, the
  SDL mapping set and ES-DE detection; they do not prove a real DS4/Xbox pad, real
  Bluetooth pairing, or the Ally's own buttons.
- The DS4 mapping is proven for the USB/HIDAPI path (the synthetic pad is USB).  A real
  DS4 over Bluetooth uses the same HIDAPI driver but was not exercised.
- `PAD-BYID` is empty in the QEMU runs: virtio-input-host gives the guest no USB/BT
  parent, so systemd's 60-persistent-input.rules sets no ID_BUS and no by-id symlink.
  The stable by-id name is expected on real USB/BT pads; it was not observed here.
- No evtest output from the Ally exists: the F15-F18 table is taken from the kernel
  hid-asus driver (drivers/hid/hid-asus.c, merged in 6.10), not measured on the device.
  `sudo evtest` on the Ally is still the acceptance check for the button table.
- The "player 1 is the pad used last" helper orders by connection recency (highest
  event number first).  It was proven with a fixture and with SDL's enumeration order,
  not with two physical pads on the Ally.
- RetroArch was not exercised in the guest.  Its default joypad driver is `udev`, which
  uses RetroArch's own autoconfig profiles, not the SDL database; the README documents
  the one-line `input_joypad_driver = "sdl2"` switch.
